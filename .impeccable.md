## Design Context

### Users
SwarmOracle serves curious, strategy-minded users exploring historical or hypothetical "What-If" scenarios through interactive simulation. They use it in a browser-first context across desktop and mobile devices, often arriving to quickly launch a scenario, inspect branching outcomes, place bets, and interact with director tools without needing onboarding-heavy flows.

### Brand Personality
Cinematic, tactical, legible.

The interface should feel like a playable speculative command room rather than a generic dashboard or a toy experiment. It should communicate confidence, immediacy, and strategic depth, while staying understandable for first-time users. i18n correctness is a product requirement, not a polish item.

### Aesthetic Direction
Keep the existing product identity: editorial landing page, simulation workspace, and Pixel Theater as the experiential core. Favor a browser-native premium feel over novelty styling. Preserve the current structure and major interactions, but sharpen hierarchy, typography, state feedback, and mobile fit so the product feels deliberate on Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, and across major browsers.

Avoid large redesigns, decorative AI-slop tropes, and visual effects that obscure state. Theater can stay atmospheric, but controls, labels, and status surfaces must remain readable and tactile.

### Design Principles
1. Preserve the game loop: scenario setup, simulation watching, prediction, intervention, and result reading must stay familiar and uninterrupted.
2. Prioritize strategic readability: make primary controls, current state, and next decisions obvious within seconds.
3. Treat i18n as a first-class constraint: Chinese and English copy must both fit, remain natural, and avoid mixed-language leakage.
4. Optimize for browser portability: interactions, spacing, touch targets, and layout must hold up across desktop and mobile viewports without platform-specific assumptions.
5. Improve quality through restraint: use small, high-confidence refinements in spacing, typography, feedback, and responsiveness instead of structural churn.
